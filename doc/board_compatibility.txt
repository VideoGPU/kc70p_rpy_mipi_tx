Board compatibility and connector interchangeability
====================================================

This file covers popular boards with MIPI CSI-2 camera inputs. For each it lists what
its camera connector requires, which other boards share the same connector (mechanically
interchangeable: the same cable fits), and whether things work out of the box
electrically: with a stock Raspberry Pi camera module, and with the planned bridge
outputs.

Assumptions about the bridge
----------------------------

- O15: RPi 15-pin 1.0 mm output, 2 lanes (FMC_MIPI port C).

- O22: RPi 22-pin 0.5 mm output, 2 lanes, or 4 lanes using FMC_MIPI ports C+D.

- Host I/O level selectable between 1.8 V and 3.3 V (I2C and GPIO).

- CAM_IO0 goes to the FPGA power-enable input (J2 93); the FPGA can treat it as
  active-high or active-low.

- CAM_IO1 can be routed to J2 95 (RST) or J2 91 (MCLK input), or left open.

- FMC_MIPI runs 800 Mb/s per lane, within every listed host whose limit is published (Pi
  3B+/4B: 2 lanes at 1 Gb/s; Pi 5: 4 lanes at 1.5 Gb/s; SL2610: 2 lanes at 1.5 Gb/s).

  Meaning: RPi 15-pin 1.0 mm FFC family: GND, D0-, D0+, GND, D1-, D1+, GND, CK-, CK+,
    GND, IO0, IO1, SCL, SDA, 3V3
  --------------------------------------------------------------------

  Meaning: RPi 22-pin 0.5 mm FFC family: GND, D0-, D0+, GND, D1-, D1+, GND, CK-, CK+,
    GND, D2-, D2+, GND, D3-, D3+, GND, IO0, IO1, GND, SCL, SDA, 3V3
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mark: Src V
  Meaning: checked in this session against the vendor document
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mark: Src S
  Meaning: from the research survey of vendor documents, not re-checked
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mark: Src I
  Meaning: inferred
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  Mark: H / M / L
  Meaning: confidence

Some vendors (Synaptics, NVIDIA Orin Nano) number the R22 connector from the 3V3 end.
The signal order along the connector is what matters, not the pin numbers.

What to do with each group
--------------------------

1. Reachable with O15 + O22 (class A/B, about 25 boards): every Raspberry Pi with a
   camera connector, Machina SL2610, Jetson Nano/Xavier NX/Orin Nano, BeagleBoard, TI
   SK, Kria KV260, STM32MP1/MP2, Radxa ZERO 3.

2. Reachable with bridge options:

   - 1.8 V host I/O: TI SK-TDA4VM/SK-AM68 (default setting), Jetson Orin Nano GPIOs,
     Radxa ROCK 5C.

   - CAM_IO1 routing: Jetson Nano/Xavier NX drive MCLK on pin 12; Radxa ZERO 3 outputs a
     clock on pin 18.

   - 2-lane mode on O22: STM32MP257F.

3. Needs a passive adapter (no electronics, only re-pinning): Coral Dev Board/Mini (24p
   to O22), Radxa ROCK 5 (vendor cables exist), Radxa ROCK 4SE/3C (vendor opposite-side
   cable). Possible later as small FFC adapter boards.

4. Needs a dedicated adapter: Rockchip/Khadas/Pine64/StarFive 30-pin boards (custom
   interposer, often 1.8 V), NXP Mini-SAS, Qualcomm, Toradex Verdin, Variscite.

5. Already covered by FMC_MIPI: Jetson TX2 (and probably AGX Xavier/AGX Orin, to verify)
   take FMC_MIPI J2 directly.

6. Out of scope: boards without a MIPI camera connector (Pi 400/500, Le Potato, BPI-M5,
   Orange Pi Zero 3, KR260, Portenta H7, Libre Tritium with parallel camera), and Libre
   Renegade Elite (5 V on the camera power pin).

7. Coralboard SL: measure the connector first. If it is R22 with 3.3 V I/O, it is in
   group 1.

Pitfalls
--------

- Same connector does not mean the same pinout: ROCK 4SE/3C and Milk-V Duo S J2 use the
  R15 envelope with reversed signals, and Libre Solitude/Alta carries a second clock
  pair on R22 pins 17/18. Compare the signal order, not the pin count.

- Pin numbering direction differs: Synaptics J206 and NVIDIA Orin Nano number R22 from
  the 3V3 end. The research survey misread the Orin Nano as "reversed" for this reason.
  NVIDIA states that an RPi v2 camera works there with a 15-to-22 cable.

- Contact side: bottom-contact (Machina J206, Orin Nano, most BeagleBoards) versus
  top-contact connectors decide whether a same-side or an opposite-side cable is needed.
  A 15-22 cable carries only 2 lanes.

- 22-pin does not mean 4 lanes: Pi Zero, the CMIO board, STM32MP257F and some RISC-V
  boards wire only 2 lanes on a 22-pin connector.

- I/O levels: some hosts use 1.8 V I2C (TI SK default, Khadas, Rockchip boards,
  Variscite, Qualcomm). Orin Nano mixes 3.3 V I2C with 1.8 V GPIO, so the bridge's
  host-side GPIO inputs must switch at 1.8 V levels.

- Camera power pin: usually 3.3 V, but Libre Renegade Elite puts 5 V (VCC_SYS) on it.
  Industrial boards often provide several camera rails (2.8/1.8/1.2 V: UP Squared,
  CrossLink-NX, Qualcomm, i.MX 93 through I2C expanders, Star64). No vendor publishes
  the camera-supply current budget.

- GPIO meaning differs: power-enable (RPi), power-down (Jetson, Coral; polarity
  differs), MCLK output (Jetson Nano pin 12), clock out (Radxa ZERO 3), second clock
  pair (Libre Alta). Many hosts drive an MCLK (Jetson, Toradex, UP, Radxa ROCK 4SE/3C,
  Pine64, StarFive). Never drive CAM_IO0/IO1 from the bridge by default, and accept or
  ignore a host clock.

- D-PHY lane order and P/N order vary: Radxa ROCK 3C/4SE reverse both, ROCK 5 reverses
  lane order, Qualcomm's Vision mezzanine mixes P/N order on one connector, CrossLink-NX
  uses CK, D3, D1, D0, D2. FMC_MIPI's lane order and polarity are fixed in copper, so
  adapters must fix them in copper. A firmware swap may be possible because FMC_MIPI
  drives each LP line separately, but it has not been tried.

- I2C jumpers: CM4 IO (CAM0) and CM5 IO (CAM/DISP1) need J6 jumpers.

- I2C topology: Jetson dual-camera carriers use an I2C mux, TI uses a TCA9543A switch
  per connector, BeagleBoard gives each connector its own bus. Some boards share the
  camera bus with an ID EEPROM or the GPIO header (BeagleV-Fire, Milk-V Duo S).

- Shared I2C buses: Machina's camera bus also carries power management, and the Pi 3B+
  camera bus is shared with the display. The bridge must never hold the bus low, and the
  emulated sensor address must be free (FMC_MIPI's EEPROM also answers at 0x54/0x5C).

- Board-to-board connectors are not camera connectors: Ultra96, RB5, Portenta X8 and
  ZCU104 expose CSI only as raw pins on a general expansion connector.

- Host software: the host needs a driver and device-tree entry for the emulated sensor.
  OV5647 (0x36) is supported by the Pi, Machina and Coralboard. IMX219 (0x10) is
  supported by the Pi, Jetson Nano/Xavier NX/Orin Nano and VisionFive 2. FMC_MIPI's
  current firmware profile is the NVIDIA E3326 (OV5693). Electrical fit and driver
  support are separate questions: Libre Alta takes an RPi camera cable but has no driver
  for it, while VisionFive 2 / Milk-V Mars have IMX219/IMX708 drivers but a different
  connector.

Unknowns
--------

- Coralboard SL camera connector: type, pin 1, pinout, I2C level. The kit photos on
  developers.google.com show it, but they could not be analysed in this session.

- The Molex part number behind Machina J206 (CON_5051102296) and its exact contact side.

- Whether FMC_MIPI J2 is pin-compatible with the Jetson AGX Xavier / AGX Orin camera
  connectors.

- Pi 5: the board-level pin table is not schematic-confirmed (RPi documentation and the
  CM5 IO datasheet agree with R22).

- Jetson Xavier NX / AGX / TX2 and NXP i.MX 8M / i.MX 93 connector details are behind
  vendor logins.

- Contact side (top or bottom) is published for only a few boards (RPi, BeagleBoard,
  Orin Nano, Machina).

- Per-lane rates at board level are mostly unpublished (known: Pi 3B+/4B 1 Gb/s, Pi 5
  and SL2610 1.5 Gb/s).

- Radxa cable wiring diagrams (JavaScript-rendered pages) and the Firefly camera I2C
  level were not available.
