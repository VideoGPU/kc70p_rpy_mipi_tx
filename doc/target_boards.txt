Target host boards
==================

The bridge must look like a camera module to these three hosts. Unless another source is
given, every fact below comes from a document in doc/vendor/ (see sources.txt
<sources.txt>). Confidence: H = read directly from a schematic or official document, M =
inferred from official material (app notes, BSP sources), ? = not published, must be
measured on the real board.

-------

What the bridge needs for all three:

- A 2-lane CSI-2 output with the RPi camera-module pinout. One FMC_MIPI port (C or D) is
  enough.

- I2C and GPIO translation from the FMC_MIPI 1.8 V side to 3.3 V hosts, and to 1.8 V
  until the Coralboard level is known: a selectable host I/O reference.

- Host 3.3 V from the camera connector routed to J2 108/110 ("host present"), and the
  power-enable GPIO routed to J2 93 (see fmc_mipi_interface.txt
  <fmc_mipi_interface.txt>).

- Only a few mA drawn from the host 3.3 V. No vendor publishes the camera-supply current
  budget; the Cameleon rule (CAM-SYS-014) is at most 10 mA per host rail, and no
  back-feeding.

- CAM_IO0/CAM_IO1 treated as inputs by default. Their meaning differs between hosts.

Pi 3B+ and Machina J206 both use the Raspberry Pi signal order, so one RPi-pinout output
reaches both:

- Pi 3B+: a 15-pin output with a standard 15-15 cable.

- Machina: a 22-pin output with a 22-22 cable, or a 15-pin output with a 15-to-22 cable.
  Synaptics' own reference camera is a 15-pin OV5647 module. Check the cable contact
  side against J206 (bottom contact) before the first power-up.

The Coralboard connector must be identified on a real board before deciding the output
for it. A 22-pin output on the bridge is also needed for 4-lane hosts (Pi 5, CM4/CM5 IO
boards). See board_compatibility.txt.

Cable facts (RPi drawings in doc/vendor/raspberry_pi/):

- Standard camera cable (RP-008146): 15 pins at 1.0 mm, 16.0 mm wide, 200 mm.

- 15/22 cable (RP-008145, "mini" cable used by Pi 5 / Zero): 15 pins at 1.0 mm (16.0 mm
  wide) to 22 pins at 0.5 mm (11.5 mm wide). It carries only 2 lanes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Raspberry Pi 3 Model B+
--------------------------

Sources: raspberry-pi-3-b-plus-reduced-schematics.pdf, rpi-doc-camera-pinouts.adoc,
rpi-doc-csi-2-usage.adoc, raspberry-pi-3-b-plus-product-brief.pdf.

- Receiver: BCM2837 "Unicam", CSI-2 D-PHY 1.1. Models before Pi 5 route only 2 data
  lanes of the second Unicam instance to the connector, at up to 1 Gb/s per lane (Pi 5
  product brief).

- Connector: 15-pin 1.0 mm FFC, TE 1-1734248-5 on the 3B+ schematic (RPi documentation:
  compatible with Amphenol SFW15R-2STE1LF). The Pi 4B uses the same pinout but leaves
  CAM_IO1 (pin 12) unconnected.

- Pin 1 (RPi documentation): with the board upright and the logo readable, pin 1 is the
  contact furthest from you, nearest the GPIO header. On a camera board held lens-down
  with the connector on the right, pin 1 is the contact nearest to you.

- The camera I2C (SCL0/SDA0, 1.8 kOhm pull-ups to 3.3 V) also goes to the DSI display
  connector.

- Software: bcm2835-unicam + a V4L2 sensor driver + a device-tree overlay. The overlay
  sets the number of lanes, continuous or non-continuous clock, and the link frequency,
  so the FPGA emulation must match one supported sensor profile (for example OV5647 at
  0x36, or IMX219 at 0x10) or come with its own driver and overlay.

- Alternative: the Pi kernel also supports two CSI-2 bridge chips, ADV728x-M (analog
  video) and Toshiba TC358743 (HDMI). If the FPGA emulated a TC358743 instead of a
  sensor, the Pi would receive RGB888 or YUV422 frames with no ISP tuning. Over 2 lanes
  the documented limits are 1080p30 RGB888 or 1080p50 YUV422. The FPGA I2C target would
  then have to emulate the TC358743 registers (timing detection, EDID) instead of a
  sensor's.

2. Synaptics Astra Machina SL2610 developer kit
-----------------------------------------------

Sources: machina-io-board-schematic.zip (sheet 06 "CONN - MIPI CSI" and sheet 13
"SODIMM"), sl2610-datasheet.pdf section 2.8, astra-doc-hw-sl2600.rst.txt,
machina-mipi-csi-camera-connection-appnote.pdf,
astra-doc-isp-sensor-integration.rst.txt.

- Receiver: SL2610 family (SL2619 on the kit). One 2-lane CSI-2 RX, up to 1.5 Gb/s per
  lane, up to 2160p30, 2 virtual channels, HDR, multidrop, ISP. The PHY runs from
  MIPI_CSI_AVDD1P8 (1.8 V) with a 200 Ohm REXT. A hardware camera-mute pin exists
  (CAMERA_MUTE, SODIMM 144). CSI pins exist on SL2613, SL2617 and SL2619; SL2611 and
  SL2615 have no CSI.

- The I/O board is shared with the SL16x0 core modules. The SL2610 core module drives
  only CSI0 RD0/RD1/RCK (SODIMM 67-81), which go to J206. The 15-pin J207 (CSI1, part
  CON15_1X15_DRB_SFW, RPi 15-pin pinout, I2C on V3P3.TW0) is fed from SODIMM 28-42,
  which are all "N/A" on the SL2610 core module, so J207 is used only with SL1680 core
  modules. Note: the camera app note's 15-pin "MIPI-CSI1" pin table names SL2619 too,
  but the schematic and the SL2600 user guide show that SL2610 reaches only J206.

- Connector J206: 22-pin 0.5 mm FPC (schematic value CON_5051102296, probably Molex
  505110-2296, not verified). The Synaptics app note inserts the cable with the
  stiffener facing up, so J206 is bottom contact. Depending on the board build it has an
  edge-rotate or a pull-push actuator.

- I2C: SM_TW0 (SODIMM 138/140, 1.8 V), level-shifted to 3.3 V on the I/O board, 33 Ohm
  series resistors. All SL2610 TWSI ports are 1.8 V natively; Synaptics recommends 2.2
  kOhm pull-ups and a level shifter for 3.3 V targets (TWSI app note). The bus is shared
  with current monitors (0x40, 0x41, 0x42, 0x45) and FXL6408 IO expanders (0x43, 0x44)
  that switch board power. The emulated sensor must not use these addresses, and the
  bridge must never hold the bus low. FMC_MIPI's EEPROM at 0x54/0x5C does not collide.

- Software: Astra SDK (Yocto). Any sensor with a V4L2 subdevice driver can be added
  through the device tree (sl2619-rdk.dts, camera on i2c0) and the ISP media-server
  configuration. OV5647 is the reference sensor.

Synaptics also offers a 22-pin FFC to "QTH" converter board (730-C01089-01) for its own

published, so it is not a known way to plug FMC_MIPI into the kit.

3. Synaptics Coralboard SL (SL2619, Grinn AstraSOM-261x)
--------------------------------------------------------

Sources: Google Coral pages (developers.google.com/coral/products/...), Synaptics
Coralboard pages, coral-product-brief.pdf, grinn-ov5647-camera-overlay.dtso (Grinn
meta-grinn-astra BSP).

- Variant: "Coralboard SL" is the SL2619-based board, designed by Grinn on the
  AstraSOM-261x module. The Limited Edition / Google I/O Edition 2 GB is this variant.
  "Coralboard SR" uses the Astra SR200 series and is not a target.

- Connector: CSI FPC between the two halves of the mikroBUS socket. Google, Synaptics
  and Grinn publish no schematic, pinout or connector part number. The kit has an OV5647
  "CSI camera module" and a "CSI flex ribbon cable"; the camera mounts on the sensor
  add-on board with M1.6 screws (Raspberry Pi camera boards use M2), so the stock camera
  is probably not an RPi-format board. The Machina J206 and Grinn's 22-pin DSI connector
  suggest a 22-pin 0.5 mm part. This is an inference only (low confidence).

- Linux (Grinn BSP overlay for AstraSOM-261x, verified file): OV5647 on i2c2 at 0x36,
  pwdn-gpios = <&porta 5 GPIO_ACTIVE_LOW> (power-enable role), 2 data lanes,
  non-continuous clock, ISP enabled. Google's user guide names the Coralboard overlays
  grinn-astra-261x-coral-hat-overlay.dtbo and
  grinn-astra-261x-coral-ws-panel-overlay.dtbo; their sources are not public.

- Software: the preloaded image is a Grinn-customised Astra SDK v2.2 OOBE image (Yocto
  5.0 "Scarthgap"). Synaptics plans Coralboard support in the regular Astra SDK later in
  2026; its SDK download page says "coming soon".

- Mechanics: the sensor add-on board (HAT) plugs on top of the Coralboard and the camera
  flex folds under it, so the CSI connector sits under the HAT. With the bridge, leave
  the HAT off or route the flex out from under it.

- Board facts: USB-C for power and host data (ADB, NCM networking); USB-A host port
  limited to about 0.6 A at 5 V; 20-pin header with a 3.3 V UART (pin 4 GND, 6
  SM_URT0_TXD, 8 SM_URT0_RXD); mikroBUS and Qwiic connectors; 3.7 V LiPo input (JST-PH
  2.0); M.2 2230 Wi-Fi/BT (AMPAK AP12611); boot switch (eMMC default).

- Availability: Limited / Google I/O Edition first, then pre-orders through DigiKey
  (Coral site, July 2026). Synaptics support
  (https://synacsm.atlassian.net/servicedesk/customer/portal/2987) is the place to ask
  for the CSI connector pinout.

- Open points to measure on a real board before connecting the bridge: which end is pin
  1, the 3V3 pin, the I2C pull-up voltage (1.8 V or 3.3 V), and the GND pins.

- Power: USB-C, from a host port or a USB-C supply.
