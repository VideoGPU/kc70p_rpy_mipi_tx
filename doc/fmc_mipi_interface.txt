FMC_MIPI J2 interface (bridge input side)
=========================================

The bridge plugs into connector J2 of the FMC_MIPI
<https://github.com/VideoGPU/FMC_MIPI> board. J2 carries the NVIDIA Jetson TX1/TX2
developer-kit camera-connector pinout: towards J2 the FMC_MIPI board looks like a Jetson
camera module (E3326-like). The bridge must look like the Jetson carrier board to
FMC_MIPI, and like a camera module to the target host boards.

Source of the data below: VideoGPU/FMC_MIPI commit 6afc7c8 (branch rev_2021_reduced; the
design files are identical on master 319e640), netlist FMC_MIPI_v2.net (KiCad 6.0.9),
sheets Jetson_connector, LVDS_to_Jetson_lines, Slow_signals_level_shifters, Power.

FPGA side (for firmware and bring-up)
-------------------------------------

FMC_MIPI J1 is a Samtec ASP-134488-01 (FMC HPC footprint). Only LA-bank pins are used.
The MIPI_CSI2_TX constraints (constrains_for_KC705.xdc) place it on the KC705 FMC HPC
connector J22: cam0_mclk is FPGA pin G22 = HPC LA31_P (UG810). KC705 VADJ_FPGA powers
the FPGA side of every FMC_MIPI level shifter. Its default is 2.5 V (1.8 V and 3.3 V are
possible; UG810).

enables the whole port C switch, and LA09_N enables the whole port D switch.

Known limitation of this FMC_MIPI revision
------------------------------------------

FMC_MIPI/review_2021.txt notes that the TS3DDR3812 switch (U5/U6) is "not good for
handling SLVS signals" (D-PHY HS). It points to XAPP1339 and suggests HD3SS3411,
TMUXHS4412, Diodes PI3WVR628 (a MIPI switch) or TS5MP645 for a future revision. The
bridge cannot fix this. It should add as little as possible to the channel (short, 100
Ohm differential, no stubs or test points on the lanes), and the lane eye should be
checked at the host connector during bring-up.

What this means for the bridge
------------------------------

CSI-2 lanes
-----------

- Two 2-lane D-PHY ports: port C (Jetson CSI2: D0 19/21, D1 31/33, CLK 25/27) and port D
  (Jetson CSI3: D0 20/22, D1 32/34, CLK 26/28). HS comes from FPGA LVDS outputs through
  XAPP894-style resistor networks (220/47/9.1 ohm), LP comes from 1.2 V CMOS drivers
  (U8, SN74AVCH16T245 at 1.2 V), and the TS3DDR3812 switches U5/U6 select HS or LP. Lane
  rate in the existing firmware (MIPI_CSI2_TX
  <https://github.com/VideoGPU/MIPI_CSI2_TX>): 800 Mb/s per lane.

- Ports C and D share their HS/LP select lines (SWITCH_DATA_LANES_CSI_CD drives SEL1 and
  SWITCH_CLOCK_LANES_CSI_CD drives SEL2 of both U5 and U6). The two ports therefore
  enter and leave HS mode together. Possible uses:

  1. one 2-lane port (C or D) to one host;

  2. the same 2-lane stream on C and D to two hosts at the same time;

  3. one 4-lane port: clock = C_CLK, lanes 0..3 = C_D0, C_D1, D_D0, D_D1 (D_CLK unused),
     provided the firmware drives both ports with the same timing.

Two independent streams with different timing are not possible without reworking
FMC_MIPI.

- Route each pair as 100 ohm differential with matched lengths, and keep the P/N
  polarity and lane order as they are (no swaps), so that every output works with the
  existing firmware.

I2C
---

- Three independent 1.8 V I2C buses: I2C_CAM (75/77), CAM_I2C2 (87/89), I2C_GP0
  (105/107). Each goes through its own TCA9406 to separate FPGA pins, so each bridge
  output connector can get its own bus (and its own emulated sensor).

- On the J2 side each TCA9406 has internal 10 kOhm pull-ups to the FMC_MIPI 1.8 V rail.
  When FMC_MIPI is unpowered the TCA9406 pins are high-impedance (Ioff <= 2 uA), so an
  unpowered FPGA stack does not load the host's bus.

- I2C_CAM also has an AT24MAC402 EEPROM on it (A2=1, A1=A0=0: EEPROM at 0x54, serial/EUI
  at 0x5C). Hosts will see these addresses. JP1 selects the EEPROM supply (FMC_MIPI 1.8
  V by default, or the host 1.8 V from J2 pin 102).

- Host-side levels differ (Raspberry Pi: 3.3 V with 1.8 kOhm pull-ups on the Pi). The
  bridge needs a 1.8 V <-> host-level translator. Use a passive pass-FET type (PCA9306,
  LSF0102 or discrete BSS138), not a second one-shot/auto-direction translator in series
  with the TCA9406. The pass-FET type also passes clock stretching.

Control and GPIO signals
------------------------

- Host -> FPGA: CAM0_MCLK (91), GPIO0_CAM0_PWR (93), GPIO2_CAM0_RST (95). They are
  A-side inputs of SN74AVCH16T245 U3 with VCCA = 1.8 V: VIH = 0.65 x 1.8 V = 1.17 V, and
  the input range is 0..3.6 V, so 1.8 V and 3.3 V host GPIOs can drive them directly
  (add a series resistor). The inputs have bus-hold; TI does not recommend pull
  resistors on bus-hold inputs.

- Proposed mapping for RPi-style connectors: CAM_IO0 (power enable) -> pin 93; CAM_IO1
  (LED / GPIO) -> pin 95 through a 0 ohm / jumper option.

- FPGA -> host: GPIO9 (112) and CAM_INTR (117) are 1.8 V push-pull outputs that the FPGA
  can tri-state (LA13_P). They need level shifting before they drive a 3.3 V host input.

Power
-----

- FMC_MIPI is powered from the FMC (KC705). It does not supply any power on J2, and it
  draws only sensing current from the host rails: 3V3 (108/110), 2V8 (81-83), 1V8 (102)
  and 1V2 (101) go through 2.2 kOhm resistors to level shifters and then to the FPGA as
  "host rail present" flags. VDD_5V (109/118/120) is not connected on FMC_MIPI.

- The bridge should connect the host 3.3 V (camera connector supply pin) to J2 108/110,
  so that the firmware can keep the D-PHY outputs disabled (EN_U5/EN_U6) until the host
  is powered. This avoids back-powering an unpowered host's D-PHY receiver through its
  ESD diodes.

- Optional: a small 1.8 V LDO from the host 3.3 V, enabled by the host's power-enable
  GPIO, can drive J2 102 (1V8 "module powered" flag, optional EEPROM supply). This
  copies how a real camera module turns on its regulators from CAM_IO0.
